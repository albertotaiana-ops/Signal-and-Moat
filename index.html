01Business Model — What Atlassian Does
Core Products & Engine
Atlassian builds the collaboration software that software teams and IT departments run on: Jira (work tracking), Confluence (knowledge), Jira Service Management (IT/service desks), Loom, Bitbucket, Trello and Rovo (AI search, agents and automation). The unifying layer is the Teamwork Graph — over 250 billion objects and relationships.

Scale: 350,000+ customers, used in ~85% of the Fortune 500 (which is only ~10% of revenue — the base is long-tail). Service Collection: 70,000+ customers and over $1B ARR. Teamwork Collection upgrades: 3,000+ customers, 7x YoY.

FY26 revenue $6.57B (+26%): Cloud $4.41B (67%), Data Center $1.83B (28%), Marketplace & other $0.33B (5%).
Economics & the Cloud Migration
Cloud is subscription revenue recognised ratably (+31% in Q1 FY27). Data Center is self-hosted software sold on term licences with a large upfront revenue recognition under ASC 606 — so it is lumpy and now guided to shrink 16% in FY27 as customers move to cloud.

The migration is partly forced: Data Center reaches end-of-life in March 2029; customers spend ~1.75x more three years after moving to cloud. Migrations contributed a mid-to-high single-digit share of cloud growth in Q1.

Margins: gross margin 85%, non-GAAP operating margin 30% in FY26 (guide 25.5% for FY27), but GAAP operating margin 0.2% in FY26 because SBC is $1.6B (24% of revenue). FCF $1.32B (20%).

Capital: $1.8B repurchased in FY26 and $588M in Q1; debt ~$1.0B; cash $1.1B.
The One-Line Debate
Is Atlassian a cloud compounder (RPO +44%, cloud +31%, FCF margin 26%, a captive Data Center upgrade cycle) or a seat-based tool exposed to AI agents (ARR growth guided down to ~18.5% from ~23%, SBC eating all the free cash flow)? The stock answered both ways in 2026: $57 (10 Apr) → $228 (23 Oct) → $154 (6 Nov).
02Competitive Moat & Adversarial Analysis
Bull Case
System-of-record for how software gets built: Jira/Confluence hold tickets, specs and decisions; the Teamwork Graph (250B+ objects) is exactly the context agents need.
Captive upgrade cycle: Data Center end-of-life (Mar 2029) forces migration; customers expand ~1.75x after moving. Management says conversations are about 'timing, not whether'.
Backlog accelerating: RPO $4.7B (+44%), cRPO +28%; $1M+ customers growing >30%, $3M+ customers >50%.
AI is being adopted, not just discussed: Rovo used by 87% of enterprise cloud customers; 10M+ monthly human-agent collaborations (2x in six months); MCP server 2M+ monthly users.
Unit economics: 85% gross margin; Q1 non-GAAP operating margin 32%, FCF margin 26%.
Conservative guidance habit: Q1 revenue $1,772M vs own guide $1,705–1,715M; cloud +31% vs ~28.5% guide.
Bear Case
Seat-based model vs AI coding agents: if agents write code, fewer engineers need Jira seats; Atlassian says usage pricing starts in December but 'is not a near-term revenue driver'.
Decelerating headline numbers: subscription ARR growth guided to ~18.5% for FY27 vs ~23% exit rate; Q2 cloud growth guided to ~27% (-4pts).
GAAP profit is ~zero: FY26 GAAP operating margin 0.2%, GAAP EPS -$0.21; FY27 GAAP margin guide ~5%.
Crowded field: Microsoft (GitHub/Azure DevOps), ServiceNow, monday.com, Asana, Linear, Notion — plus AI-native planning tools.
Billings below expectations ($1.50B, +22%) and 47-month customer-acquisition payback (StockStory).
Data Center cliff: $1.83B of revenue shrinking 16–30% a year for several years must be replaced by cloud first.
Pre-Mortem — What Would Permanently Impair the Business
AI coding agents cut developer headcount at core customers by 20–30% while agent workflows route around Jira; seats shrink, usage pricing is too small to compensate, cloud growth falls below 10% by FY29 and SBC stays at ~15% of revenue. Owner free cash flow never exceeds ~12% of revenue and fair value is ~$51 (-67%) — close to the April low of $57. Probability: ~25%.

Where the Cash Actually Lands
The biggest 'supplier' is the workforce: $1.6B of stock-based compensation in FY26, 24% of revenue and larger than free cash flow ($1.32B). The company then spends cash to buy back stock ($1.8B in FY26, more than FCF) to offset dilution. For shareholders, the cash that 'lands' is therefore roughly zero after SBC — which is exactly the number the market's multiple depends on.
Chain Risk Flags
Model vendors: Rovo relies on third-party models that also compete for the same 'work context'.
Hyperscaler cost: 85% gross margin could compress as agents consume inference.
Channel: Marketplace partners could lose relevance if agents replace plugins.
Talent: SBC is the price of keeping engineers; cutting it risks execution.

Quality of Earnings
The non-GAAP story (30% margin, $5.85 EPS) is real operationally, but GAAP operating income was $10M in FY26. The gap of $1.99B is SBC ($1.61B), restructuring ($280M) and acquired-intangible amortisation ($101M). Treat SBC as a real cost: FCF minus SBC was about -$0.29B in FY26.
Capital Return vs Cash Generation
FY26 buybacks of $1.8B exceeded FCF of $1.32B; Q1 FY27 buybacks of $588M exceeded FCF of $463M. Cash fell from $1.24B to $1.12B while debt stayed ~$1.0B. The buyback offsets SBC dilution — it is not 'excess' capital return.

Why the Stock Fell on a Beat
• Exit-rate vs guide: ARR growth of ~23% in Q1 versus ~18.5% guided for the full year implies sharp deceleration.
• Cloud growth steps down ~4pts in Q2.
• FY27 guide was raised by less than the Q1 beat (ASC 606 timing on Data Center plus ~$20M of cloud beat not passed through).
• Billings +22% were below expectations.
• The stock was down 24.5% in the prior month and had run +300% from April — positioning was stretched.
Management Tone
Confident on AI, careful on numbers. CEO Mike Cannon-Brookes: 'Put us to the test'; agents increase the value of coordination. CFO James Chuong calls the guide 'prudent'. Atlassian's record of beating its own guide is strong, so some of the implied slowdown is likely conservatism — but the guide is what the market priced.

GAAP profit is ~zero

Non-GAAP operating margin falls from 30% (FY26) to 25.5% (FY27 guide) — Q1's 32% implies ~23% for the remaining three quarters.

Data Center accounting

Buybacks > FCF
$1.8B in FY26 and $588M in Q1 FY27 exceeded FCF; cash drifted down.
AI pricing is not yet revenue
Usage-based Rovo pricing starts in December but 'not a near-term revenue driver' — optionality, not estimates.
Volatility regime
$57 → $228 → $154 in seven months: sentiment, not fundamentals, drives much of the move; position sizing matters.

The SBC Question — the Whole Debate in One Number
If you ignore SBC and value Atlassian on reported free cash flow, the same base case is worth about $226 (+47%). If you count SBC as the real cost it is, the value is $136 (-11%). The $90-per-share gap is the entire disagreement between bulls and bears. We take the stricter view, as in the Salesforce analysis, because a 24%-of-revenue SBC load is a recurring transfer from shareholders to employees, not a footnote.
Contrarian View
The market punished a 31%-cloud-growth, 44%-RPO-growth business for guiding one quarter conservatively — and the bulls are right that Atlassian has a record of beating its own guide. The contrarian twist is that it still isn't cheap: after a 16% drop, you are paying ~27x guided non-GAAP EPS and ~27x FY27E FCF for a company whose GAAP profit is near zero. The better entry is probably lower — near $115 — or a later print that shows ARR growth holding above 20%. Great business, fair-to-full price after the drop.
